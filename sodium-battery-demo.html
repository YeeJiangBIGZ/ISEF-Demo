Experiment roadmap — from walnut shell to battery test

Full workflow, step by step — the highlighted step 7 is our core treatment. Click any card to expand what happens and why. Scroll sideways → to see every step.

1

Raw walnut shells

Collect & clean

photo

What happens: waste walnut shells are collected and physically cleaned — the inner membrane, leftover kernel and dirt are stripped away.

Purpose: obtain clean, impurity-free biomass before any chemical or thermal steps.

click to expand
2

Citric-acid leaching

Acid washing

photo

What happens: shells are soaked in a citric-acid solution to dissolve the mineral ash (K, Ca, Mg carbonates) trapped inside the shell pores.

Purpose: remove ash that would otherwise cause side reactions and high impedance in the finished battery.

click to expand
3

Rinse & oven drying

Rinsing · drying to constant mass

photo

What happens: residual acid is rinsed out of the pores until the rinse water is neutral (pH≈7), then the shells are oven-dried to constant mass.

Purpose: remove leftover acid (protects the furnace) and fully dry the shells so they grind cleanly.

click to expand
4

Ball-mill grinding

Fine powder · 100–200 mesh

photo

What happens: the dried walnut-shell pieces are ground in a planetary ball mill into a fine, uniform powder (100–200 mesh).

Purpose: control particle size so carbonization and foaming happen uniformly.

click to expand
5

Carbonization (tube furnace)

Inert gas · 800 °C

photo

What happens: the powder is heated to 800 °C under high-purity argon (5 °C/min, hold 2 h) inside a tube furnace.

Purpose: pyrolyze the biomass into black, amorphous hard carbon with the disordered structure that stores sodium.

click to expand
6

Sample grouping

C0 · C15 · C25 · C35

photo

What happens: the carbon powder is split into 4 batches — C0 (untreated control), C15, C25 and C35 — ready for foaming treatment.

Purpose: isolate foaming pressure as the single variable so its effect on pore structure can be measured.

click to expand
7 ★ Core innovation

Supercritical CO₂ foaming

Core treatment · 15 / 25 / 35 MPa

photo

What happens: the C15 / C25 / C35 samples are loaded into the supercritical reactor and treated with CO₂ at 15 / 25 / 35 MPa.

Purpose: tune the internal pore structure of the hard carbon by adjusting foaming pressure — this is the main variable we study.

click to expand
8

Material characterization

SEM · BET · XRD

photo

What happens: SEM (morphology), BET (pore volume & surface area) and XRD (carbon disorder) are measured, with Raman as an option.

Purpose: see how foaming pressure changes pore size and distribution — the project's core structural evidence.

click to expand
9

Electrode & CR2032 half-cell

Fabrication · coin-cell assembly

photo

What happens: working electrodes (carbon : CMC : SBR = 90 : 5 : 5 on Cu foil) are made, then CR2032 Na half-cells are assembled in a glovebox with 1 M NaPF₆ in DME.

Purpose: build real sodium-ion half-cells to test how the carbon stores and releases sodium.

click to expand
10

Electrochemical testing

GCD · CV · EIS

photo

What happens: galvanostatic cycling (GCD), cyclic voltammetry (CV) and EIS are run to measure capacity and initial Coulombic efficiency (ICE).

Purpose: quantify reversible capacity, plateau capacity and ICE to test the scientific hypothesis.
